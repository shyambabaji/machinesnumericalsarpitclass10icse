Q5. Block & Tackle System
A block and tackle system has 5 pulleys. If an effort of 1000 N is needed in downward direction to raise a load of 4500 N, calculate:
(a) the mechanical advantage,
(b) the velocity ratio, and
(c) the efficiency of the system.
📌 Given Data
• No. of pulleys (n) = 5
• Effort (E) = 1000 N
• Load (L) = 4500 N
(a) Mechanical Advantage (M.A.)
Step 1: Write formula for M.A.
M.A. = L / E
Step 2: Substitute given values.
M.A. = 4500 N / 1000 N
Step 3: Perform division.
M.A. = 4.5
(b) Velocity Ratio (V.R.)
Step 1: For a block and tackle system, V.R. is equal to total number of pulleys (n).
V.R. = n
Step 2: State value.
V.R. = 5
(c) Efficiency (η)
Step 1: Write formula relating η, M.A., and V.R.
η = (M.A. / V.R.) × 100%
Step 2: Substitute values.
η = (4.5 / 5) × 100%
Step 3: Simplify fraction and multiply.
η = 0.9 × 100%
η = 90%

Q7. Ideal vs Practical Pulley System
A pulley system has a velocity ratio 3. Draw a diagram showing the point of application and direction of load (L), effort (E) and tension (T). It lifts a load of 150 N by an effort of 60 N. Calculate its mechanical advantage. Is the pulley system ideal? Give reason.
📌 Given Data
• V.R. = 3
• Load (L) = 150 N
• Effort (E) = 60 N
Step 1: Calculate Mechanical Advantage (M.A.)
M.A. = L / E
Substitute values:
M.A. = 150 N / 60 N
M.A. = 2.5
Step 2: Check Efficiency & Ideal Nature
Calculate Efficiency (η):
η = (M.A. / V.R.) × 100%
η = (2.5 / 3) × 100% = 83.33%
No, the pulley system is NOT ideal.
Reason: For an ideal system, M.A. must be equal to V.R. (η = 100%). Here, M.A. (2.5) < V.R. (3) due to friction in pulley bearings and the non-zero weight of lower movable parts.

Q8. Four-Pulley System Analysis
Fig. 3.32 shows a system of four pulleys. The upper two pulleys are fixed and the lower two are movable.
(a) Draw a string around the pulleys. Also show the point of application and direction in which the effort E is applied.
(b) What is the velocity ratio of the system?
(c) How are load and effort of the system related?
(d) What assumption do you make in arriving at your answer in part (c)?
📌 Solutions & Answers
(a) Diagram Description
String is tied to the hook of the lower block, passed around upper and lower pulleys alternately, with the free end pulled downwards delivering Effort (E).
(b) Velocity Ratio (V.R.)
Total number of pulleys in system n = 4.
V.R. = n = 4

(c) Relation Between Load (L) and Effort (E)
Step 1: Tension T acts upwards in 4 supporting strands.
L = 4T
Step 2: Effort applied equals tension in free end.
E = T

L = 4E   or   M.A. = L / E = 4
L = 4E
(d) Assumptions Made
1. The string is weightless (massless) and perfectly inextensible.
2. There is no friction in the pulley bearings/grooves.
3. The weight of the lower movable block is negligible.

Q10. Efficiency & Maximum Load
A block and tackle system has velocity ratio 3. Draw a labelled diagram of the system indicating the points of application and the directions of load L and effort E. A man can exert a pull of 200 kgf.
(a) What is the maximum load he can raise with this pulley system if its efficiency is 60%?
(b) If the effort end moves a distance 60 cm, what distance does the load move?
📌 Given Data
• V.R. = 3
• Max Effort (E) = 200 kgf
• Efficiency (η) = 60% = 0.60
(a) Maximum Load Raised (Lmax)
Step 1: Calculate Mechanical Advantage (M.A.).
η = M.A. / V.R. ⇒ M.A. = η × V.R.
M.A. = 0.60 × 3 = 1.8
Step 2: Calculate Maximum Load (L).
M.A. = L / E ⇒ L = M.A. × E
L = 1.8 × 200 kgf
Lmax = 360 kgf
(b) Distance Moved by Load (dL)
Step 1: Given dE = 60 cm, V.R. = 3.
V.R. = dE / dL ⇒ dL = dE / V.R.
Step 2: Substitute values.
dL = 60 cm / 3
dL = 20 cm

Q11. Multiple Movable Pulleys (M.A. = 8)
You are given four pulleys and three strings. Draw a neat and labelled diagram to use them so as to obtain a maximum mechanical advantage equal to 8. In your diagram mark the directions of load L, effort E and tension in each strand. What assumptions have you made to obtain the required mechanical advantage?
[Hint: Three movable pulleys with one fixed pulley attached to the same support].
📌 Solution & Derivation
System Configuration
• Number of movable pulleys n = 3
• Number of fixed pulley = 1
• Formula for V.R. of n movable pulleys with separate strings:
V.R. = 2n = 23 = 8
Assumptions Made
1. The weight of all 3 movable pulleys is negligible (zero).
2. There is no friction in any of the pulley bearings.
3. All three strings are weightless, inextensible, and perfectly flexible.

Q13. Comparative Design Selection
Two students submit different pulley designs A and B to lift a load of 2500 N with maximum effort 500 N and efficiency 80%. Design A has a V.R. of 5 and design B has a V.R. of 7. Calculate the effort for each design and justify which design is better.
📌 Given Data
• Load (L) = 2500 N
• Max Limit of Effort = 500 N
• Efficiency (η) = 80% = 0.80
• Design A: V.R.A = 5 | Design B: V.R.B = 7
Step 1: Calculate Effort for Design A
M.A.A = η × V.R.A = 0.80 × 5 = 4
EA = L / M.A.A
EA = 2500 N / 4
EA = 625 N
Step 2: Calculate Effort for Design B
M.A.B = η × V.R.B = 0.80 × 7 = 5.6
EB = L / M.A.B
EB = 2500 N / 5.6
EB ≈ 446.43 N
Step 3: Justification & Conclusion
• Maximum effort user can supply is 500 N.
• Design A requires 625 N effort (> 500 N), so it FAILS to lift load.
• Design B requires ~446.43 N effort (≤ 500 N), which is within effort limit.
Design B is BETTER and PRACTICAL.

Q14. Combined Pulley & Lever System
To lift a heavy load of 600 N vertically, load is attached to a block and tackle system of 4 pulleys [2 pulleys each]. The free end of rope is tied to a uniform lever of length 2 m pivoted at one end at a distance of 0.5 m from the fulcrum. A man applies effort E at the other end of lever. Assume the system to be ideal. Calculate:
(a) The mechanical advantage of the pulley system and tension T to support the load.
(b) The least value of effort E applied to lift the load.
(c) Is there any advantage of attaching a lever to the pulley system?
📌 Given Data
• Load on pulley (L) = 600 N
• No. of pulleys in block & tackle (n) = 4
• Pulley efficiency η = 100% (Ideal)
• Lever length = 2 m (Pivoted at one end ⇒ Effort Arm E.A. = 2 m)
• Rope attached at distance from fulcrum ⇒ Load Arm (L.A.) = 0.5 m
(a) Pulley M.A. and Tension (T)
Step 1: For ideal 4-pulley system:
M.A.pulley = n = 4
M.A.pulley = 4
Step 2: Relation between Load and Tension (L = 4T).
T = L / 4
T = 600 N / 4
Tension (T) = 150 N
(b) Least Value of Effort E Applied
Step 1: Load force acting on lever = Tension from rope (T) = 150 N.
Step 2: Apply Principle of Moments for lever:
Effort (E) × Effort Arm (E.A.) = Load on lever (T) × Load Arm (L.A.)
Step 3: Substitute values.
E × 2 m = 150 N × 0.5 m
E × 2 = 75
E = 75 / 2
Effort (E) = 37.5 N
(c) Advantage of Attaching Lever
YES, HUGE ADVANTAGE!
• M.A. of lever alone = E.A. / L.A. = 2 / 0.5 = 4.
• Total Mechanical Advantage = M.A.pulley × M.A.lever = 4 × 4 = 16.
It reduces effort required from 150 N down to just 37.5 N (4 times reduction).
